# Fatwa Archive — How to help

As-salāmu ʿalaykum wa rahmatullāhi wa barakātuh.

You only ever need **three links**. Bookmark them. You never need to open Drive folders,
rename files, or know how anything works behind the scenes.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Upload link — Send files** | You have videos or audio on your phone or computer. Send up to 20 at once. |
| **Link 1 — Submit a link** | You found a video online (YouTube, a website). Fill one short form. |
| **Link 2 — My tasks** | Your list of transcripts and translations to work on. |

*(All links are pinned in the group. Ask the admin if you don't have them. Keep the
upload link inside the group — it works for anyone who has it.)*

---

## If you have videos or audio files

This is the usual case: clips forwarded on WhatsApp, saved on your phone.

1. Open the **upload link**. Type your name once — it is remembered.
2. Say which scholar, how sure you are, and where you got them. This applies to every
   file; you can change it for one file if needed.
3. Tap **Choose videos or audio** and pick up to 20 from your gallery.
4. Each file is checked straight away:
   - **Already in the archive** — the exact same file is there. Nothing to do; it was
     not uploaded twice.
   - **Possibly already in the archive** — the same recording, but a different file (a
     forward, a different cut). Listen to both, then choose **Same recording** or
     **Different recording**. A scholar giving the same answer on another occasion *is*
     a different recording — send it.
   - **No sound that could be read** — a photo or a screen recording. Remove it.
5. Tap **Send**. Done.

Send them within a day: files that were checked but never sent are removed after 24
hours.

## If you found a video online

1. Open **Link 1**.
2. Fill the form. About one minute, mostly dropdowns.
3. Press submit. That's it — you're done.

The most important field is **the link to where you found it**. Never leave it empty.
If you genuinely can't remember where it came from, say so in the form. An honest gap is
usable; a guess is not.

If you are **not sure the speaker is really that scholar**, choose *Not sure* in the form.
This is not a weakness — it is exactly right. A fatwa attributed to the wrong scholar is
the worst mistake this project can make, so we would rather have one entry less.

You don't need to upload the file — a link is enough. If you have the file but no link,
use the upload link instead.

## If you have a task (transcript or translation)

1. Open **Link 2**. You see your rows. Each row has two buttons: **▶ Audio** and **📄 Doc**.
2. Click **📄 Doc**. It opens in your browser. You will see a table:

   | Time | Arabic | English | German |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 0:08 | السؤال: ما حكم المسح على الخفين؟ | The question: what is the ruling on... | |
   | 0:14 | الجواب: المسح على الخفين ثابت... | The answer: wiping over the... | |

3. A machine-made draft is already there. **You correct it — you never start from nothing.**
   Work in your column only. Listen along with ▶ Audio.
4. When you're finished, go back to **Link 2** and change your status cell to
   **Done – waiting for review**.

Everything saves automatically. There is nothing to download, nothing to upload, and
nothing to name.

## The rules that matter

**Never add or delete table rows.** Every row is tied to a second in the audio. If you
remove a row, the link between the text and the recording breaks, and someone clicking a
quote lands at the wrong place in the recording. If a row is genuinely empty, leave it
empty.

**Correct, don't improve.** The Arabic is a word-for-word record of what was said, not an
essay. Keep the repetitions, keep the filler words, keep the order. Fix only what the
machine misheard — names, Qur'an and hadith quotes, and technical terms are where it is
wrong most often.

**Translate close, not beautiful.** A clumsy sentence that is accurate beats a smooth one
that shifts the meaning.

**Keep the ruling words exact.** *harām*, *makrūh*, *mustahabb*, *wājib*, *sunna*, *mubāh*
are not interchangeable. Keep the Arabic word and put a short gloss in brackets:

> …is makrūh (disliked, but not forbidden)

Flattening *makrūh* into "forbidden" turns a dislike into a prohibition. This is the single
most damaging translation mistake, and it happens quietly.

**Keep conditions as conditions.** "If X, then Y" must never become a general ruling.

**Add nothing the scholar didn't say.** Not even an explanation you think is helpful.
If you think something needs clarifying, write it as a note — see below.

## When you're unsure — mark it, don't decide

| Write | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[...]` | Can't make out what is being said |
| `[?]` | After a word: this is my best guess, please check |
| `[note: ...]` | A message to the reviewer. Never published. |

Qur'an verses: put them in the Arabic brackets `﴿ ﴾` instead of normal quotation marks.
This lets us link the verse automatically later.

Marking something costs the reviewer twenty seconds. A quiet wrong guess may never be
caught at all. Ten marks too many is better than one too few.
